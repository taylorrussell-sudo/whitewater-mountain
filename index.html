Understanding the dashboard

Simulation methodology, source attribution and the roadmap.

Weather sources

    Times are shown in Pacific time (Whitewater). Last checked is when the service completed a request. Valid time belongs to the observation or forecast hour. Exact model issue times are unavailable. Forecast freshness describes retrieval age. Stale values are excluded from active indicators.

    Refresh Data checks eligible sources. One request per minute and up to six early weather batches per UTC day. Whitewater snow checks keep a two-hour minimum and station checks keep a one-hour minimum. The road camera keeps its 15-minute minimum. Provider stops and backoff apply to all checks.

    ECMWF IFS HRES drives the extended outlook and powder alerts. Canadian HRDPS is independent short-range guidance. Snow bins accumulate over complete hours, starting at the next whole hour. Missing bins suppress affected totals and alerts.

    The GFS freezing outlook lists every resolved crossing or exact-zero altitude band above model terrain. Forecast inversions use matching model hours. Observed comparisons use fresh Whitewater Base and Nelson CS temperatures within 30 minutes. Resort timestamps are interpreted as Pacific time because the source leaves the offset unspecified.

    01 / Elevation & freezing level

    The 0°C level is a forecast atmospheric elevation. It is not the existing snowpack boundary or a reliable rain/snow boundary. Inversions can break a simple vertical temperature relationship. The profile is schematic, not a trail map. Elevations are planning values; verify Glory's lower terminal and Nordic trailhead before live release.

    02 / Powder alert logic

    Blue solid ring = reported snowfall; violet dashed ring = forecast snowfall. Alerts use 24-hour totals, not multi-day accumulation. Forecast alert uses the largest modeled rolling 24-hour total in the next 72 hours; the time window is shown. The glow begins to pulse slowly at 15 cm. Reduced motion keeps the glow static. These thresholds are initial defaults, not official resort definitions.

    03 / Data & prediction methodology

    Observed = measured or officially reported. Forecast = provider model. Calculated = our interpretation. Unavailable = missing, invalid or stale. This simulation uses fictional values. Future models will be kept separate, including differences in elevation and precipitation type. Forecast agreement is not a calibrated probability.

    05 / Temperature inversion indicators

    The dashboard subtracts the Nelson valley temperature (~535 m) from the Whitewater Lodge temperature (~1,630 m). Forecast comparisons use matching valid times. Live observations can differ by up to 30 minutes. Missing or mismatched pairs are unavailable.

    Lodge minus valleyDashboard signal
    0°C or lessNo positive inversion signal
    Above 0°C and below 3°CWeak
    3°C to below 6°CNotable
    6°C or greaterStrong

    These provisional thresholds describe the station difference for this dashboard. Meteorological inversions concern how air temperature changes with height. The two sites are at different locations, and local conditions can affect their temperatures. Two readings cannot establish the vertical temperature structure or the cold layer’s height, depth, or shape. The hatched band is a schematic cue for a positive observed signal.

    Circles show simulated observations. Diamonds show the forecast pair with the largest lodge-minus-valley difference, using the earliest pair for ties. Forecast hours are relative to the scenario start. Consecutive comparable samples three hours apart can bracket onset and dissipation of the positive run containing the peak. When both transitions are bracketed, the duration range spans the time between the first and last positive samples up to the time between the surrounding nonpositive samples.

    The inversion comparison is independent of the forecast 0°C level, powder alerts, and avalanche ratings. An inversion can complicate the relationship between elevation and freezing. Precipitation type depends on the full atmospheric profile.

    What this could mean for skiing

    Subzero air can be milder at the lodge than in the valley. Wind and clothing affect comfort. Above-freezing air can contribute to snow softening or thaw. Snow history, radiation, wind and precipitation also affect the snow surface. The station difference alone cannot predict powder quality, sunshine, visibility, road conditions or avalanche danger.

    The inversion example includes a mock observation of valley fog and clear lodge skies at scenario start. The ski context reports the valley-fog and clear-lodge contrast when valid sky readings match the temperature pair in time and type. The forecast sky comparison is unavailable in the demo. The dissipation bracket estimates when the station-temperature difference becomes zero or negative. Valley cloud or inversions at other elevations may persist.
